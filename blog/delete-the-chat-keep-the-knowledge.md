# Delete the chat. Keep the knowledge.

Bao Trinh · 14 October 2026

This week, out of every ten Claude chats I opened, I deleted two or three myself.

Not because they were useless — because they ended up confidently wrong, and I know the next chat will search my history, find that wrong answer, and build on it. Deleting is the only way I have to protect the next chat. It also deletes every correction I made inside the bad one.

That is the quiet cost of working with an AI assistant at scale. The output is good. What survives between chats is not — and if you use Claude Code, transcripts older than thirty days are deleted for you on launch, by default, with no log. So I wrote project instructions, and they rotted. I wrote a summary at the end of each chat to carry into the next, and after a hundred of them I could not tell a right one from a wrong one. One project ended with 103 handoff files, several duplicated on the same day.

So I built a knowledge ledger for Claude. Every chat reads from it and writes into it; my data stays where it is. The AI proposes a claim; a human signs it — an AI never signs. A claim becomes knowledge, a [T], only with truth — evidence anyone can open; trust — the name that signed it; traceable — the ids it rests on. Until then it is information, held as an [A] with my name on it and the condition that would settle it. Retire a premise or withdraw evidence, and every [T] standing on it falls back to [A] — needs review. Nothing quietly stays true; nothing is thrown away.

The chat is disposable now. The knowledge is not.

Do you get this with Claude too: waste time, irritation of explaining again and again, and helplessness? 😇

Sources of the numbers: “two or three out of ten this week” — the author's own count; “103 handoff files” — the author's project folder; “thirty days” — Claude Code's default transcript retention, as reported by [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/a/5264673).
