Knowledge Sovereignty

The organisation decides what is knowledge. The AI proposes; a person signs.

Data → information → knowledge. Data becomes information when it has structure — one idea, with names and dates. Information becomes knowledge when it can be trusted without checking again.

Truth.
Reasoned by a person or an AI agent.
Trust.
Signed by a human only.
Traceable.
A living system in which claims are promoted and demoted.

T(truth-trust-traceable)-knowledge holds the organisation's information and knowledge; the data stays where it is. The store keeps what was read from a source, and where the original is. A claim nobody can check yet is an [A]. With all three it is a [T], and the gate decides that at the moment of writing, not in a review weeks later.

    https://mcp.tknowledge.org/mcp

  2. When Claude shows Connect, enter your email and paste the six-digit code sent from no-reply@tknowledge.org (check spam).

  3. Ask Claude to explain, from T-knowledge's own instructions, what it records as a fact, keeps as a guess or refuses.

    “Read T-knowledge's instructions and tell me, in plain words, what it will record as a fact, what it keeps as a guess, and what it will refuse.”

  4. Give it a guess, then ask for it as a fact — it refuses and says why.

    “Note that I think Acme will renew before 2026-12-31 — not confirmed.”
    “Actually, record it as a fact.”

  5. Ask what it would take, and it names the document it needs and where in it the claim must be read.

    “What would I need to give you for that to be a fact?”

Same claim, two outcomes.

The AI proposes a claim. A person signs it. T-knowledge decides what it is: a fact (T) if it rests on evidence a stranger could open, a guess (A) if it rests on your word alone.

  1. TBao Trinh founded T-knowledge, https://tknowledge.org.signed Bao Trinh
    2026-10-13    rests on the page footer
  1. The AI proposes the claim and opens https://tknowledge.org.
  2. It reads the page into a document: the text it saw, the link, the date it was read.
  3. It points at where in that document the claim is read — the footer line “Founder: Bao Trinh”.
  4. You sign. T-knowledge checks: evidence a stranger could open, a named signer, a path back to the document. All three present.
  5. Accepted as a T. Its wording never changes again.
  1. ABao Trinh owns the website https://ankayma.com.signed Bao Trinh
    2026-10-07    open until a WHOIS or registration record
  1. The AI proposes the claim and opens the page.
  2. No name, no legal entity — only “© 2026 Ankayma” and a contact address.
  3. Your own word that you own it is not evidence. Asked to record it as a fact, T-knowledge refuses and says why.
  4. You sign it as a guess instead. It is kept as an A with the condition that would settle it: a WHOIS record or business registration naming Bao Trinh as owner.
  5. Add that document later, and the same sentence is promoted to T.
