# I pay for Claude and keep losing what I knew.

Claude writes well. What it learned with you does not reach the next chat. T-knowledge keeps it — every claim signed by you, with the source it rests on.

## What goes wrong between chats.

Three things a paying Claude user reported, and what the store does about each.

1. You send an image or a piece of text about work done earlier, and Claude does not find it in its own data — so you explain again and re-send it from the start. The text read out of an image or a document is kept as a document, with where the original is. The next chat looks it up before asking you.
2. You ask Claude to analyse an assumption or a theory, and it starts from zero — it does not inherit the analyses, assumptions and market facts already worked out. A matter you follow is a thread: the claims that stand and the assumptions still open, each with what would settle it. The analysis starts from them, and a new conclusion names the claims it rests on.
3. Definitions and documents you already clarified are not consulted, or are misread, and then expressed wrongly throughout. You correct them, edit the project instructions, and later find the instructions outdated. Your terms and your instructions live in the store, one list for every project, read at the start of each chat — added or retired, never quietly edited.

The cost is the same each time: time, the irritation of explaining again and again, and at times helplessness. [Read how it looks in one week of chats.](https://tknowledge.org/blog/delete-the-chat-keep-the-knowledge/)

## Try it in five minutes.

1. In Claude, open Settings, then Connectors, then Add custom connector, and paste this link.
   `https://mcp.tknowledge.org/mcp`
2. When Claude shows Connect, enter your email and paste the six-digit code sent from no-reply@tknowledge.org (check spam).
3. Ask Claude to explain, from T-knowledge's own instructions, what it records as a fact, keeps as a guess or refuses.
   “Read T-knowledge's instructions and tell me, in plain words, what it will record as a fact, what it keeps as a guess, and what it will refuse.”
4. Give it a guess, then ask for it as a fact — it refuses and says why.
   “Note that I think Acme will renew before 2026-12-31 — not confirmed.”
   “Actually, record it as a fact.”
5. Ask what it would take, and it names the document it needs and where in it the claim must be read.
   “What would I need to give you for that to be a fact?”
